UHC 2.0 · A proposed calendar system

A calendar for the Human Era.

The Universal Human Calendar (UHC) is a proposed way to organise civil dates around a shared human timeline: thirteen equal months, a fixed historical epoch, and a continuous seven-day week.

01 / The idea

What is the Universal Human Calendar?

The Universal Human Calendar is an alternative civil calendar proposal. It gives dates a year number counted from a shared reference point in the distant human past, then organises each year into thirteen equal 28-day months.

Its aim is practical as well as symbolic: make month lengths predictable, provide one continuous year count, and offer a calendar that can be converted to and from the Gregorian calendar.

What the epoch means: 10,000 BCE is a chosen calendar starting point near the beginning of the Holocene and major transitions towards settled ways of life. It is not a claim that human beings or civilisation began on one precisely known day. The epoch is fixed so dates do not move when archaeological knowledge changes.

Thirteen months. Four weeks each.

03 / The extra days

Days outside the months

Thirteen 28-day months total 364 days. The solar year is longer, so UHC adds one World Day every year and one Leap Day in leap years.

World Day · WD

World Day is the final day of every UHC year. It follows December 28 and is not assigned to a month.

Leap Day · LD

Leap Day occurs after June 28, but only in leap years. Sol begins the following day.

The seven-day week never pauses. World Day and Leap Day are counted as real days in the continuous Monday-to-Sunday sequence. They are outside the month structure, not outside time or outside the week.
04 / Leap years

How the calendar stays aligned

UHC 2.0 uses the Gregorian leap-year rule. A Human Era year is a leap year when it is divisible by 4, except century years that are not divisible by 400.

Leap year ⇔ (Y mod 400 = 0) OR ((Y mod 4 = 0) AND (Y mod 100 ≠ 0))

05 / The week

A continuous seven-day cycle

UHC retains Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday in their uninterrupted sequence. Intercalary days receive the next weekday just like any other day.

+1

After an ordinary year

The next year's first weekday advances by one because the year has 365 days.

+2

After a leap year

The next year's first weekday advances by two because the year has 366 days.

7

Always in sequence

No day is removed from the weekday cycle. Month starts therefore do not remain on one fixed weekday.

This is a deliberate UHC design choice. Some other 13-month calendar proposals place extra days outside the seven-day week to keep month weekdays fixed. UHC does not do that; preserving the continuous week takes priority.
06 / Date conversion

How Gregorian dates become UHC dates

The year number is shifted by 10,000, but the month and day are recalculated. Because UHC months are all 28 days long, conversion uses the date's position within the year (its ordinal day), not the original Gregorian month and day.

1

Convert the year

For CE dates, add 10,000. For BCE dates, use HE year = 10,001 − BCE year.

2

Find the ordinal day

Count the date's position from January 1 in the corresponding proleptic Gregorian year.

3

Insert Leap Day when needed

In a leap year, ordinal day 169 is Leap Day. Later dates shift by one ordinal position in UHC.

4

Map the remaining days

Divide the regular sequence into 13 blocks of 28 days. The final day of the year is World Day.

Example · Ordinary year

8 November 2026 has ordinal day 312. In UHC, ordinal day 312 is day 4 of month 12.

2026-11-08 → HE12026-12-04

Example · Leap year

17 June 2028 is ordinal day 169, the inserted Leap Day. The following day begins Sol.

2028-06-17 → HE12028-LD
2028-06-18 → HE12028-07-01
07 / Notation and time

Dates change. Timekeeping does not.

UHC changes the civil calendar used to name a day. It does not redefine the second, the 24-hour day, time zones, or Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). A timestamp still identifies a time of day and an offset from UTC.

Year, month and day are written with a clear calendar prefix.

Intercalary date

Special dates use their own identifiers, rather than pretending to belong to a month.

HE12026-12-04T12:30:45+05:30

The example means 12:30:45 at UTC+05:30 on the fourth day of the twelfth UHC month in 12026 HE. The converter can also show the corresponding UTC time.

UHC is not UTC. UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is an international time standard. UHC (Universal Human Calendar) is a proposed calendar for numbering and organising civil dates. UHC uses existing timekeeping standards rather than replacing them.
08 / Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is UHC an official international calendar?

No. UHC 2.0 is a proposed calendar design and an experimental converter. It has not been adopted as an official civil calendar by governments or international standards organisations.

Did human civilisation begin exactly in 10,000 BCE?

No. There is no single universally agreed birthday for civilisation. The epoch is a fixed convention chosen near the beginning of the Holocene and major transitions in human settlement. It is not a precise archaeological claim.

Does every UHC month begin on the same weekday?

No. Every month has exactly four weeks, but the weekday cycle continues through World Day and Leap Day. Since those extra days count in the week, the weekday at the start of a year changes from year to year.

Why is there a month called Sol?

Sol is the added thirteenth month. It is numbered 7 and sits between June and July, so July to December become months 8 to 13. The name is a short reference to the Sun and distinguishes it from the familiar names of the other twelve months.

Can religious and cultural calendars still be used?

Yes. UHC is designed as a proposed civil date system. Religious, cultural, lunar and regional calendars can continue to be used independently.

Can all historical dates be converted by adding 10,000?

Only the year number follows that simple offset. Month and day must be recalculated from the day of the year. Dates originally recorded in another calendar, such as the Julian calendar, must first be interpreted in that calendar and converted to an equivalent Gregorian date.

About this proposal: UHC 2.0 is a community-developed calendar concept. Its epoch, month structure, intercalary-day placement and notation describe this proposal; they are not an existing internationally ratified standard.
